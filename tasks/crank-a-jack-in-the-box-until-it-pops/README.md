# Crank a jack-in-the-box until it pops

Turn the offset handle on a closed jack-in-the-box until the figure pops out, then stop and release the crank. This takes a continuous circle around a small axle, with a stable grasp through changes in direction. The pop is an immediate visible cue to stop. The manufacturer describes this way of playing with the [Schylling Jester Jack-in-the-Box](https://schylling.com/product/jester-jack-in-box/).

## Real setup

Use an ordinary hand-cranked tin jack-in-the-box with a spring-up cloth figure, about 14 cm on each side and 0.5 kg. The crank axis is about 9 cm above the counter and the free-spinning knob center is about 2.6 cm off-axis. The crank extends 4–5 cm out from the right side as viewed by the robot, with a knob around 16 mm across. Set it upright on a clean tabletop, case center 30.4 cm forward and 4.6 cm left of the arm-base midpoint. The axle runs left–right. Empty space above the toy lets the figure emerge and the lid open fully.

Close and latch the lid over the figure before each run. Start with the crank knob directly below its axle. On the physical toy, use a taped dot or hand-drawn mark on the casing to establish the starting crank angle. The model cam releases after 2.85 forward turns. To reproduce a comparable physical start, let the toy pop, close it again, and count the turns to its next release in a dry run. Start future trials at the same point in the toy's cycle. Turn count can vary across physical toy models. Both grippers start open at the ready pose.

## Score

Use an ordinary video showing the full crank and lid. Mark the case position on the table at the start and use a ruler for final shift. A quarter-turn can be read from the marked crank and the axle center in video.

- **0**. Less than one net forward turn and the lid remains latched. Opening the cover by hand earns only the independent crank progress, if any.
- **0.25**. At least one full net forward turn, with the figure still inside.
- **0.75**. The crank releases the latch and the figure rises, but the arm keeps turning more than a quarter-turn after the lid first starts to lift, stays on the crank, or shifts the case more than 2.5 cm.
- **1.0**. The crank releases the latch, the lid opens at least 70° and the figure rises visibly out of the box. The crank moves no more than a quarter-turn after the lid first starts to lift. Both grippers clear the knob and the case rests within 2.5 cm of the starting position for one second.

In simulation the gear train and music are omitted. The offset crank, free-spinning knob, lid and figure move on physical joints. The crank joint has light damping. The latch opens after the crank turns far enough. Lid and figure then extend on spring drives. The robot has to exert torque through real contact to turn the crank. The scorer uses actual crank motion and checks the resulting lid angle and figure rise.

The RoboCurve task [TK-A4CVBG](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/2574) starts with the figure out and measures pushing the spring-loaded figure and lid down to latch. This task starts latched and measures winding a freely turning offset crank and stopping when its latch opens.

## Run

Use [yam-isaac-lab-arena](https://github.com/kairosiann/yam-isaac-lab-arena) with Isaac Lab-Arena. All geometry is generated by the task and uses no third-party assets.

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted \
  -E task=crank_a_jack_in_the_box_until_it_pops.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
  -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "Turn the crank until the figure pops out. Stop turning, release the crank, and leave the box where it is." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 1200 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/jack-in-the-box
```

Switch to the `dream` policy for the model run. Set `JACK_SHORT=1` with `yam_scripted` for the shorter-than-one-turn check. The simulation resets the scene, with no random seed needed when `object_jitter=0`.

In the scripted trial the left gripper braces the front of the case below the lid. The right grips the free-turning knob, follows its circular path, holds it briefly after the figure pops, and lets go. Both grippers withdraw. The trial scored 1.0 at step 389 (19.45 seconds simulated, about 104 seconds elapsed on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000). The shorter-than-one-turn control scored 0.0. Dream-1 scored 0.0 at the 1,200-step limit.
