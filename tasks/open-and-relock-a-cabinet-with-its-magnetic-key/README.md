# Open and relock a cabinet with its magnetic key

Use the supplied magnetic key against the outside of the closed door. Pull the handle while the hidden hook is
lifted, swing the door open, take the key away and shut the door so it catches again. Tug the handle once to verify
that the hook holds, then leave the key on the bench. The key works through the wood. Its release depends on
position and orientation, and removing it lets the hook return under spring load. The open, close and test sequence
follows the [manufacturer's operating guide][manual].

## Setup

A compact hardwood-veneered cabinet is anchored from the back to model an installed cabinet rigidly attached to
the wall. It is approximately 380 mm wide, 250 mm deep and 292 mm high. The flush door itself is 351 × 268 mm,
with an 18 mm wood panel and a vertical hinge at its right edge as seen by the robot. The centre of the black
round handle is 320 mm from the hinge and 86 mm above the cabinet floor. Its outer grip is 27 mm across with
17.5 mm of axial grip depth. The inside of the door has a spring-return plastic hook with a steel armature. The
matching keeper is screwed into the top of the cabinet. The hook's bevel lets the door cam shut and catch.
The lock armature is behind the upper middle of the door, 188 mm from the hinge and 257 mm above the floor.

The key is a 42 mm diameter, 38 mm tall plastic-and-magnet assembly (54 g). Its flat magnet face lies on the bench.
The blue stem is up. In metres from the arm bases' midpoint, with x away from the robot and y to its left, the
hinge is at (0.406, −0.183) and the key starts at (0.238, −0.151). Both grippers start open. For each repeat
return the key to its mark on the bench, close the door and check that the hook starts engaged.

## Scoring

A real trial can be scored from an overhead video, a side view of the door seam and a ruler or angle gauge.
Use the same key every time. A proper opening uses the key on the outer face and reaches at least 28° with an
undamaged catch. It earns 0.5. Closing so the hook re-engages and placing the key back within 48 mm of its starting
point earns 0.75 if both hands release. For 1.0, the handle must also resist a tug lasting at least 0.20 s after the key is
away, then remain closed for one second with both hands withdrawn. An opening forced past the catch or a failure
to open earns zero.

In the simulation the hook must be behind the keeper with its tooth down. The door must stay at most 2.3° open
while tugged (under 14 mm of gap at the free edge) and the keeper must carry 1.6–35 N for at least 0.20 s. With the grippers released, the closest TCP
must be at least 75 mm from the handle and 65 mm from the key. The benchmark observes those final conditions for
one second for full credit. This force measurement checks the physical resistance of the hook and keeper after
closing, independently of the nominal hinge angle.

The hinge, spring hook, keeper and the handheld key use rigid bodies and contact. The latch moves from torque on
its armature, calculated from the key's measured distance and face orientation. A capped inverse-fourth-power falloff represents attraction. The model uses a 0.75 N force cap and
a 0.039 N·m/rad return spring. The hook's bevel and holding face are shaped collision geometry. The magnetic reaction
on the handheld key and the adhesive backing are omitted.

## Reproduce

Install [yam-isaac-lab-arena][arena] with Isaac Lab-Arena on a GPU. All scene geometry is generated by the task
files. Use the fixed reset below. No random seed is required.

```bash
task=open_and_relock_a_cabinet_with_its_magnetic_key.py
instruction="Use the magnetic key near the upper middle of the door to open the cabinet. Remove the key before closing the door, then tug the handle to check that it locked. Leave the key on the bench and let go."

inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted \
  -E task="$task" -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "$instruction" --scorer task_score --max-steps 1200 --store-frames --log-dir ./cabinet-demo

inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task="$task" -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "$instruction" --scorer task_score --max-steps 1200 --store-frames --log-dir ./cabinet-dream
```

The trial renderer is `inspect-robots video` applied to each resulting trial JSON. `MAGKEY_DEBUG=1` reports
the hinge and latch angles, magnet field and keeper contact load. The scripted command with `MAGKEY_MISS=1`
holds the same key 63 mm off the lock and tries the same opening motion. A trial takes a few minutes wall
time on one RTX PRO 6000. Dream-1 uses configured Dream credentials and can run for 60 simulated seconds.
